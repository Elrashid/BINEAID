## **Understanding key drivers afecting students' use of ** **artifcial intelligence-based voice assistants**

## **Abstract**

Artifcial intelligence (AI)-based voice assistants have become an essential part of our daily lives. Yet, little is known concerning what motivates students to use them in educational activities. Therefore, this research develops a theoretical model by extending the technology acceptance model (TAM) with subjective norm, enjoyment, facilitating conditions, trust, and security to examine students' use of AIbased voice assistants for instructional purposes. The developed model was then validated based on data collected from 300 university students using the PLS-SEM technique. The results supported the role of enjoyment, trust, and perceived ease of use (PEOU) in afecting the perceived usefulness (PU) of voice assistants. The empirical results also showed that facilitating conditions and trust in technology strongly infuence the PEOU. Contrary to the extant literature, the results indicated that subjective norm, facilitating conditions, and security did not impact PU. Similarly, subjective norm and enjoyment did not afect PEOU. This research is believed to add a holistic understanding of the key drivers afecting students' use of voice assistants for educational purposes. It ofers several theoretical contributions and practical implications on how to successfully employ these assistants.

**Keywords** Artifcial intelligence · Voice assistant · Human-AI interaction · Technology acceptance · Drivers · Education

## **1** **Introduction**

The prevalence of artifcial intelligence (AI) applications in today's era allows individuals to interact and communicate with new non-human agents, such as voice assistants and robots. The literature witnessed an increasing interest in AI-based voice assistants, particularly in the last few years. Siri (from Apple), Cortana (from Microsoft), Alexa (from Amazon), Bixby (from Samsung), and Google Assistant (from Google) are some of the most frequently used AI-based voice assistants that are embedded in smartphones or smart speakers (McLean & Osei-Frimpong, [2019)](#page-18-0). These assistants have changed the way in which users search for information, use content, manage home automation devices, purchase products, and achieve tasks through exhibiting voice as the primary interface (Al-Kaisi et al., [2021)](#page-17-0). Through relying on AI-based algorithms, voice assistants record human speech, upload it to the cloud, parse the recording into commands, and send back the output to the user via a computer-generated message (Gouda et al.,[ 2021](#page-18-1)). A recent report indicates that 27% of the global online population is using voice assistants on their smart mobile devices (McCue, [2018](#page-18-2)). To bridge the aforementioned research gaps, this study goes a step further by developing a theoretical research model for understanding the students' actual use of voice assistants for educational purposes in a new collectivistic context. The developed model extends the technology acceptance model (TAM) (Davis, [1989](#page-17-1)) with other external variables, including subjective norm, enjoyment, facilitating conditions, trust, and security. ## **2** **Theoretical framework and research hypotheses**

By analyzing the existing literature on AI-based voice assistants, it has been noticed that there is a scarcity of knowledge concerning what impacts the use of these technologies for educational purposes. Therefore, this research develops a research model based on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and extended with several factors that are believed to afect the students' use of voice assistants in instructional activities. The selection of TAM as the basis in this research stems from several reasons. First, while it was developed in 1989 (Davis, [1989)](#page-17-1), a recent study showed that the model is still valid and that the number of TAM-based studies is still progressing on a yearly basis (Al-Emran & Granić, [2021](#page-17-2)). Second, there is little empirical support on how TAM can be employed to evaluate the use of AI-based voice assistants.

Figure [2](#page-3-0) shows the developed research model. It is suggested that perceived usefulness (PU) and perceived ease of use (PEOU) are infuenced by subjective norm, enjoyment, facilitating conditions, and trust. It is also anticipated that PU is infuenced by security and PEOU. The behavioral intention (BI) to use voice assistants is afected by both PU and PEOU, which in turn, afects the students' actual use of those assistants in higher education.

## **2.1** **Subjective norm**

Subjective norm (SN) refers to "an individual's perception of how important others in his or her social environment wish or expect him or her to behave in a certain way" (Moan & Rise, [2006)](#page-18-5). The defnition of subjective norm is also used to represent social infuence (Bloemendaal, [2018)](#page-17-3). Earlier research claimed that subjective norm impacts the perceived usefulness and ease of using voice assistants (Moriuchi, [2019;](#page-18-6) Song, [2019)](#page-19-4). Accordingly, the following hypotheses are put forward:

**H1** Subjective norm positively infuences the perceived usefulness of voice assistant technology.

**H2** Subjective norm positively infuences the perceived ease of use of voice assistant technology.

## **2.2** **Enjoyment**

Enjoyment is defned as "the activity of using a specifc system is perceived to be enjoyable in its own right, aside from any performance consequences resulting from system use" (Venkatesh, [2000](#page-19-5)). When the user feels that using a particular device is enjoyable, he/she would have positive feelings toward that device and would be more likely to use it (Basak et al.,[ 2015)](#page-17-4). The simplicity of starting the system or how efective the voice technology in delivering simple responses improves the user's enjoyment (Sorensen, [2019)](#page-19-6). The extant literature showed that enjoyment is a strong predictor of the perceived usefulness and ease of use (Al-Qaysi et al., [2021;](#page-17-5) Chu, [2019](#page-17-6); Liu et al.,[ 2021](#page-18-7)). Thus, the following hypotheses are proposed:

**H3** Enjoyment positively infuences the perceived usefulness of voice assistant technology.

**H4** Enjoyment positively infuences the perceived ease of use of voice assistant technology.

## **2.3** **Facilitating conditions**

Facilitating conditions (FC) refers to "the degree to which an individual believes that an organizational and technical infrastructure exists to support the use of the system" (Venkatesh et al.,[ 2003](#page-19-7)). The use of any technology requires specifc resources, skills, and infrastructure (Albanna et al., [2022](#page-17-7)). In this context, FC refers to the attributes such as accessibility and efciency of smartphones and other supporting technologies that impact the usage of voice assistants. Prior research endorsed the role of FC in infuencing the perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use (Bloemendaal, [2018;](#page-17-3) Li et al., [2021)](#page-18-8). Consequently, this leads to the following:

![A3.R05 fig08](../figures/A3/A3.R05_fig08.jpg)

<!-- page 6 -->

**H5** Facilitating conditions positively infuences the perceived usefulness of voice assistant technology.

**H6** Facilitating conditions positively infuences the perceived ease of use of voice assistant technology.

## **2.4** **Trust**

Trust is described as "a psychological state comprising the intention to accept vulnerability based upon positive expectations of the intentions or behavior of another" (Rousseau et al., [1998)](#page-19-8). In this context, trust is viewed as the users' beliefs towards whether the voice assistants are secure in protecting their personal data (Chu, [2019)](#page-17-6). The existing literature showed that trust has positively afected the perceived usefulness and ease of using voice assistants (Alharithi, [2019](#page-17-8); Chu, [2019](#page-17-6); Zeng, [2020)](#page-20-0). Thus, the following hypotheses are suggested:

**H7** Trust positively infuences the perceived usefulness of voice assistant technology.

**H8** Trust positively infuences the perceived ease of use of voice assistant technology.

## **2.5** **Security**

Security (SE) refers to "users' perspectives toward the protection level against the potential threats" (Park et al., [2017](#page-19-9)). Security is believed to be a signifcant factor in infuencing the use of voice assistants. This stems from the fact that those technologies typically gather information from their users, such as users' names, ages, genders, and voice recordings, to provide them with the best user experience. Previous studies showed that security signifcantly impacts perceived usefulness (Chu, [2019;](#page-17-6) Neumann, [2018)](#page-18-9). Therefore, the following hypothesis is formulated:

**H9** Security positively infuences the perceived usefulness of voice assistant technology.

## **2.6** **Perceived ease of use**

Perceived ease of use (PEOU) is defned as "the degree to which a person believes that using a particular system would be free from efort" (Davis, [1989)](#page-17-1). Users must have the impression that technological innovations in their workplace are simple to operate (Chu, [2019](#page-17-6)) and efort-free in performing the intended tasks (Lin & Chen, [2015](#page-18-10)). PEOU is found to be the fundamental element that afects the perceived usefulness and the behavioral intention to use voice assistants (Moriuchi, [2019](#page-18-6); Song, [2019](#page-19-4); Sorensen, [2019)](#page-19-6). Therefore, the following hypotheses are proposed:

<!-- page 7 -->

**H10** Perceived ease of use positively infuences the perceived usefulness of voice assistant technology.

**H11** Perceived ease of use positively infuences the behavioral intention to use voice assistant technology.

## **2.7** **Perceived usefulness**

Perceived usefulness (PU) refers to "the degree to which a person believes that using a particular system would enhance his/her job performance" (Davis, [1989)](#page-17-1). PU is considered to be the most motivational factor in IT adoption (Basak et al., [2015)](#page-17-4). When the user intends to use new technology, his/her intention is proved to be infuenced by the usefulness of that technology (Cacho-Elizondo et al.,[ 2012](#page-17-9); Chu, [2019)](#page-17-6). The existing literature supported the relationship between PU and the users' behavioral intention (Chai et al., [2020;](#page-17-10) Elfeky & Elbyaly, [2021;](#page-17-11) Liu et al., [2021)](#page-18-7). Therefore, the following hypothesis is suggested:

**H12** Perceived usefulness positively infuences the behavioral intention to use voice assistant technology.

## **2.8** **Behavioral intention**

Behavioral intention (BI) refers to the degree of intensity of users' intention to perform a particular behavior (Davis, [1989)](#page-17-1). Users who use voice assistants would have an optimistic behavior if the assistant recognized their voices and replied accurately to their requests (Sorensen, [2019)](#page-19-6). Prior research showed a strong association between behavioral intention and the actual use of a specifc technology (Afonso, [2019](#page-17-12); Mohammadi,[ 2015](#page-18-11)). Based on that, the following hypothesis is formulated:

**H13** Behavioral intention positively infuences the actual use of voice assistant technology.

## **3** **Methodology**

To examine the students' use of AI-based voice assistants, we have collected data from students studying at four private and public universities in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) using online questionnaire surveys. The targeted participants are those who adapted the voice assistant technology. A compulsory fltering question was added at the beginning of the survey to be ticked by the actual users of voice assistants for educational purposes. As there is no sampling frame for such types of users, we have employed the purposive sampling technique for collecting responses from those universities. ## **4.1** **Participants' demographics**

The participants' demographic characteristics are shown in Table [2](#page-8-0). It can be seen that 81% were females, while 19% were males. It is imperative to mention that females represent the vast majority of students in the four targeted universities. The majority of participants were in the age group of 18-21, with 57%. This is followed by 44.7% who use Siri, 4.3% who use Alexa, 3.7% who use other assistants, and 0.7% who use Cortana.

## **4.2** **Common method bias**

To ensure that the collected data did not contain common method bias (CMB), Harman's single-factor with nine variables (subjective norm, enjoyment, facilitating conditions, trust, security, perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, behavioral intention, and actual use) was conducted (Podsakof et al.,[ 2003](#page-19-17)). The nine variables were then loaded into a single variable. The analysis results showed that the largest variance explained by the single variable is accounted for 21.39%, which is below the threshold value of 50% (Podsakof et al.,[ 2003](#page-19-17)). This indicates that the collected data do not raise any issues concerning CMB.

## **4.4** **Structural model assessment**

We have tested the collinearity between the factors before evaluating the structural model to ensure no lateral collinearity issues (Ramayah et al., [2018)](#page-19-18). The variance infation factor (VIF) is one of the standard measures used for assessing collinearity. As per the readings in Table [7](#page-13-0), all the VIF values are less than 5 (Hair et al., [2011)](#page-18-14), indicating no multicollinearity. The assessment of the structural model is performed through a bootstrapping procedure of 5000 re-samples by measuring the *t*-values, standard beta (*β*), coefcient of determination (R^2^ ), efect size (*f* 2 ), and predictive relevance (*Q*^2^ ) (Hair et al., [2017)](#page-18-18). R^2^ is a common indicator for measuring the structural model and a predictor for all the endogenous variables in the research model. It has been argued that the R^2^ values above 0.67 are categorized as "High", values between 0.33 and 0.67 are categorized as "Moderate", and values between 0.19 and 0.33 are categorized as "Weak" (Chin, [1998](#page-17-15)). The results indicated that the R^2^ values for the actual use (0.638), perceived ease of use (0.645), and perceived usefulness (0.667) are within the range of 0.33 and 0.67, which means that the predictive power of the variables is "Moderate". Further, the R^2^ value for the behavioral intention to use AIbased voice assistants is discovered to be 0.763, which implies that the predictive power of the variable is "High".

Table [7](#page-13-0) shows the path coefcient results for all the proposed hypotheses in the conceptual model. According to the data analysis, 8 out of the 13 hypotheses were supported in this research, while the rest were not accepted by the empirical data. In that, the fndings demonstrated that subjective norm has an insignifcant positive infuence on perceived usefulness (*β*=0.090, *t* = 1.387) and perceived ease of use (*β*=0.017, *t* = 0.315) of voice assistants. Hence, H1 and H2 are rejected. The results showed that enjoyment has a signifcant positive infuence on perceived usefulness (*β*=0.350, *t* = 5.780), while it has an insignifcant positive impact on perceived ease of use (*β*=0.104, *t* = 1.127). Thus, H3 is accepted, and H4 is rejected.

The results indicated that facilitating conditions has an insignifcant efect on perceived usefulness (*β* = -0.109, *t* = 1.156), while it has a signifcant positive efect on perceived ease of use (*β*=0.567, *t* = 8.124). This means that H5 is rejected and H6 is accepted. Trust is shown to have a signifcant positive infuence on perceived usefulness (*β*=0.155, *t* = 2.574) and perceived ease of use (*β*=0.183, *t* = 2.397). Therefore, H7 and H8 are accepted. The results revealed that security has an insignifcant positive correlation with perceived usefulness (*β*=0.068, *t* = 1.553), which indicates the rejection of H9.

The fndings also pointed out that perceived ease of use has a positive association with perceived usefulness (*β*=0.401, *t* = 6.051) and behavioral intention (*β*=0.450, *t* = 9.614), which means that H10 and H11 are accepted. In addition, perceived usefulness has a strong positive correlation with the behavioral intention to use (*β*=0.488, *t* = 10.772), which shows the acceptance of H12. The results also indicated that the behavioral intention has a signifcant positive infuence on the students' actual use of voice assistants (*β*=0.799, *t* = 29.279). Thus, H13 is accepted.

## **5** **Discussion**

The rapid growth of information technologies has changed the way through which students communicate at "anytime anywhere" settings (Al-Emran et al.,[ 2018](#page-17-18); Alshurideh et al., [2020](#page-17-19)). AI-based voice assistants have exposed signifcant progress, and their potential is improving. While the existing literature witnessed several studies on the applications of voice assistants in diferent instructional activities, little is known concerning the determinants afecting the students' use of those assistants (Kim et al., [2020)](#page-18-20). Hence, this study aimed at examining the key factors that are believed to infuence students' use of AI-based voice assistants in educational activities. In that, we developed a research model by extending the TAM with other external factors, including subjective norm, enjoyment, facilitating conditions, trust, and security.

![A3.R05 fig17](../figures/A3/A3.R05_fig17.jpg)

<!-- page 15 -->

The research fndings showed that subjective norm has an insignifcant impact on perceived usefulness and ease of use of voice assistants. However, this contradicts with the results found in prior research (Moriuchi, [2019;](#page-18-6) Song, [2019)](#page-19-4). This might be caused by the people surrounding the students, who fnd the technology hard to use, or those who are not familiar with voice assistants specifcally for learning activities. Furthermore, the outcomes revealed that enjoyment has a signifcant positive infuence on the perceived usefulness of voice assistants, which confrms the fndings observed in earlier research (Al-Qaysi et al.,[ 2021](#page-17-5); Chu, [2019;](#page-17-6) Liu et al., [2021)](#page-18-7). This indicates that students fnd the voice assistant as an enjoyable tool, which enhances their learning performance.

While the previous literature supported the relationship between enjoyment and perceived ease of use (Al-Qaysi et al., [2021;](#page-17-5) Chu,[ 2019;](#page-17-6) Liu et al.,[ 2021)](#page-18-7), the research fndings pointed out that enjoyment has an insignifcant infuence on the perceived ease of use of voice assistants. This shows that students fnd the voice assistants hard to use due to their complexity. Therefore, it can be argued that students did not fnd any enjoyment while using the system in their learning process. Contrary to what has been observed in the existing literature (Bloemendaal,[ 2018](#page-17-3); Li et al., [2021)](#page-18-8), the results of this study did not support the correlation between facilitating conditions and perceived usefulness. This might be attributed to the reason that students have less knowledge and skills in using voice assistants in their instructional activities, which hinders the usefulness of those tools in such activities. However, the results revealed that facilitating conditions positively impact the perceived ease of use of voice assistants, which aligns with the results found previously (Bloemendaal, [2018;](#page-17-3) Li et al.,[ 2021)](#page-18-8). This indicates that fnding assistance regarding the use of voice assistants from others would facilitate the use of those assistants in instructional activities.

While 41% of voice assistant users have concerns about trust (Perez, [2019](#page-19-19)), the research fndings found that trust has a positive infuence on perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use of voice assistants in learning activities. This confrms the results found in previous studies (Alharithi, [2019;](#page-17-8) Chu, [2019;](#page-17-6) Zeng, [2020](#page-20-0)). This indicates that voice assistants would be useful and user-friendly if students fnd them reliable while searching and gathering information related to their studies. Further, the results showed that security has no signifcant impact on the perceived usefulness of voice assistants, which contradicts the outcomes observed in earlier studies (Chu, [2019](#page-17-6); Neumann,[ 2018](#page-18-9)). This means that students might consider the voice assistants useless if they expose their personal information without authorization and threaten their privacy.

In line with the TAM assumptions (Davis,[ 1989)](#page-17-1) and the conclusions drawn in previous studies (Moriuchi, [2019;](#page-18-6) Song, [2019](#page-19-4); Sorensen, [2019](#page-19-6)), the results found that perceived ease of use has a signifcant positive impact on perceived usefulness and the behavioral intention to use voice assistant technologies. This indicates that the higher the easiness of voice assistants, the higher the advantages of getting students' attention to realize the usefulness of those assistants in performing learning activities and the intention to use them in the future. The research fndings also showed that perceived usefulness has a signifcant positive correlation with the behavioral intention to use voice assistants, which comes in line with the conclusions drawn in prior studies (Chai et al.,[ 2020](#page-17-10); Elfeky & Elbyaly,[ 2021](#page-17-11); Liu et al.,[ 2021](#page-18-7)). This shows

![A3.R05 fig18](../figures/A3/A3.R05_fig18.jpg)

that students' behavioral intention is increased for those who found the technology useful in their learning activities. The results demonstrated that behavioral intention strongly infuences the actual use of voice assistants, which supports the results found in previous studies (Afonso, [2019;](#page-17-12) Mohammadi, [2015)](#page-18-11). This indicates that the actual use of voice assistants for educational purposes would increase if students exposed higher positive intentions toward those assistants.

## **6** **Conclusions**

## **6.1** **Theoretical contributions**

Stemming from the empirical data analysis, this research provides four theoretical contributions. First, this research developed an integrated theoretical model by extending the TAM with several external factors that are believed to infuence the students' use of AI-based voice assistants for instructional purposes. Unlike the previous studies that mainly examined the use of voice assistants in the western individualistic contexts (e.g., (Fernandes & Oliveira, [2021;](#page-17-20) Pitardi & Marriott, [2021](#page-19-20))), the developed model was tested in a collectivistic society like the UAE. This is believed to add a thorough understanding of the determinants afecting the use of voice assistants in unexplored contexts, such as the UAE, and open the door for further research in the domain. Second, this research contributes to the extant literature on AI-based voice assistants in general and the educational sector in specifc, which is still in its infancy stage. While the study used the TAM, which has been widely used in the past, understanding the underlying TAM constructs and the other external factors in examining the use of voice assistants in learning purposes is believed to add a signifcant value to the TAM on one side and the research domain of voice assistants on the other side.

Third, most of the extant empirical studies have focused on the consumers' use of voice assistants (e.g., (Fernandes & Oliveira, [2021](#page-17-20); Vimalkumar et al., [2021)](#page-19-3)) and ignored their role in educational activities. Thus, engaging the students in this research and understanding the factors afecting their use of voice assistants is believed to add value to the educational technology literature. Fourth, the developed model explained 63.8% of the variance in the actual use of voice assistants, which proves that the model is suitable for new collectivistic contexts like the UAE.

## **6.2** **Practical implications**

This research ofers several practical implications. First, decision-makers can rely on the fndings of this research to better understand the determinants afecting students' use of voice assistants to efectively use these technologies for learning purposes. Second, given the insignifcant role of subjective norm in afecting the usefulness and ease of use of voice assistants, decision-makers need to organize awareness campaigns and training sessions to guide students on how to use those assistants in learning activities.Ensuring and maintaining specifc guidelines for using those assistants would socially communicate their benefts among the students.

![A3.R05 fig19](../figures/A3/A3.R05_fig19.jpg)

<!-- page 17 -->

Third, the empirical results did not support the role of security in infuencing the usefulness of voice assistants by students. This is mainly because those assistants need to collect a massive amount of data from users to perform competently and provide the best user experience. Students can expose their personal information only when they feel that the information is secure and the service provider is trustworthy. Therefore, service providers need to be transparent on how the data are collected and used. Further, practitioners need to educate students about their privacy and how to protect their personal information. Fourth, stemming from the nature of voice assistants in collecting users' data, educational institutions need to have strong policies and procedures to deal with students' data breaches.

## **6.3** **Limitations and future work**

This study is limited in several ways. First, the developed model was tested on data collected from university students only, without considering educators' responses. Further research might look into the determinants afecting the use of voice assistants from the lenses of educators. Second, the developed model was based on extending the TAM with external factors and did not consider the role of moderating variables, such as gender and age, in examining the use of voice assistants. Examining the role of moderators is believed to add a signifcant value for potential work in the future. Third, this research relied on a quantitative approach through the use of questionnaire surveys, which does not allow an in-depth exploration of students' use of voice assistants. Further trials are encouraged to employ an in-depth mixed method to draw a better and deeper exploration of the topic.

Fourth, this study is cross-sectional, and therefore, a time-lag research approach is suggested to discover whether the responses of diferent participants of similar age at diferent points in time would change or not. Fifth, this research did not include the course through which voice assistants were used, which is due to the nature of the study in examining the general educational purposes of these assistants. Therefore, it is suggested to investigate the role of the course in future studies to understand how voice assistants are used across diferent courses. Sixth, the selection of participants in terms of the educational level was uneven. Therefore, future studies should consider this issue to approach neutral samples from various educational levels.
