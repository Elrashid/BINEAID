## Enhancing student satisfaction and academic performance through school courtyard design: a quantitative analysis

Muna Salameh<sup>a</sup>, Basim Touqan<sup>b</sup> and Abubakr Suliman<sup>c</sup>

<sup>a</sup>Department of Architecture, College of Architecture, Art and Design, Ajman University, Ajman, United Arab Emirates; <sup>b</sup>Department of Electrical Engineering, Faculty of Engineering and IT, The British University in Dubai, Dubai, United Arab Emirates; <sup>c</sup>Faculty of Business, The British University in Dubai, Dubai, United Arab Emirates

## **ABSTRACT **

Traditionally, students' overall satisfaction with their educational experience was typically measured using a single question or a straightforward 'yes' or 'no' response. However, this approach might not capture their complete assessment of other aspects of satisfaction. The design of School Courtyard is expected to be one of these aspects, and it will be the subject of this quantitative research. To gather the data, a self-administered survey was made available to a sample of secondary school students from two private schools. To investigate and validate the connection between School Courtyard Design, Courtyard Suitability and Courtyard Attractiveness with Students' Satisfaction, three hypotheses were developed. To evaluate the viability of the developed hypotheses, software namely SPSS (Statistical Package for Science Software) was used to carry out several tests, including regression analysis and correlation coefficient. The results showed a high relationship significance between the School Courtyard Design and Students' Satisfaction with a chance of 37.5%. Student satisfaction can be predicted also by Courtyard Suitability with a 22.9% chance. Finally, Students' Satisfaction can be additionally predicted by the Courtyard Attractiveness with a 24.7% chance. The research reveals the courtyard design needs, enhancing student satisfaction and academic performance, benefiting architects, experts, and practitioners.

## Introduction

Learning has been recognized as the foundation of economic growth and social development. The most crucial resources for achieving personal or organizational objectives are learning and the development of competence and skills. However, achieving the objective of active learning is not simple or obvious (Niemi, 2002). The arguments in (Glickman & Scally, 2008) state that strong communities with healthy economies depend on having quality schools. It can be argued that the desire to create a school building that increases educational attainment is on par with the significance of social and economic development. Designing schools well in terms of layout, architecture, space, services and facilities can make them resemble workplaces that value quality and productivity (Samad & Macmillan, 2005). Schools are purpose-built organizations according to obvious goals known to all and the extent to which established organizations succeed in fulfilling their objectives can be assessed. In practice, the effectiveness of an institute in attaining its educational goals can be analysed in association with its design (Coleman, 1995). The system of offering education has undergone significant

According to (Samanta, [2022](#page-17-1)), today, not only the classrooms but also the courtyards, playgrounds, theatres, and many other spaces, are structures that built with standards and regulations that support students' growth in all domains, whether it is physical or cognitive. Courtyards in schools as the main part of its architecture are increasingly in the spotlight because of their usefulness and socialization (El-Samea, Hassan, & Abdallah, [2020)](#page-16-3). Particularly in hot and arid climates, they are popular for their distinctive bioclimatic effectiveness and natural ventilation. Natural ventilation is one of the most important energy-saving techniques for lowering energy usage in buildings (Beccali, Strazzeri, Germanà, Melluso, & Galatioto, [2018)](#page-16-4). A courtyard promotes wind flow in the summer, which creates a good ventilation system that keeps the body cool and comfortable. Additionally, it blocks the winter winds, which enhances the effect of retaining heat during the chilly winter (Shi & Fan, [2014)](#page-17-2), Ghorbani Naeini et al. [(2023)](#page-16-5), Teshnehdel, Soflaei, and Shokouhian ([2020](#page-17-3)). Furthermore, a successful educational system depends on student satisfaction, and having quality schools is related to some extent to the school courtyard. It has been debated by (Brown & Mazzarol, [2009](#page-16-6)) that student satisfaction and loyalty are a reflection of the host institution's image. Therefore, a well-designed courtyard could be part of a school image and has a couple of roles that lead to obtaining student satisfaction. Research on the topic of school courtyards and their appropriateness and effect for in schools has gained significant attention in various studies. The research by (Elgheznawy & Eltarabily, [2021)](#page-16-7) investigated the application of sun sails to enhance the outdoor thermal comfort within school courtyards, with a specific focus on El-Safwa School in Egypt. The research in (Mahmoud & Abdallah, [2022](#page-16-8)) delved into passive strategies for mitigating outdoor thermal discomfort in schools situated in hot, arid climates. It proposed the integration of hybrid shading combined with the planting of trees within canyons to diminish heat stress and elevate the comfort of students. (Cantón, Ganem, Barea, & Llano, [2014](#page-16-9)) claimed that level of shading in courtyards has a substantial influence on the thermal and energy conditions within classrooms and this highlights the efficacy of passive strategies for interior space conditioning. It can be argued that most of the studies about school courtyards were related to their environmental behaviour while very limited studies were conducted on them concerning their impact on educational achievement that needs to be studied and highlighted. Evidence shows that there have been very few studies on the impact of school courtyards on learning in the United Arab Emirates, making this a promising and fruitful area for further study.

In the educational landscape, investigating the impact of school courtyard design on students' satisfaction, motivation, and overall well-being is paramount. This research consolidates our inquiries into three key questions to shed light on the complex relationship between school courtyard design and students' satisfaction:

- . To what extent does school courtyard design, including suitability, decoration, and thermal comfort, impact students' satisfaction, learning motivation, and overall well-being in UAE educational settings?
- . How can statistical analysis techniques, such as regression and correlation analysis, quantify the influence of school courtyard design on students' satisfaction, learning motivation, and wellbeing?
- . What strategies and design principles can educational institutions implement in their courtyard spaces to optimize the physical and psychological well-being of students, ultimately enhancing their educational experience in the UAE?

## Normal distributed test

Numerous statistical techniques have been put forth to examine the normality of data in a variety of ways. They vary according to the size of the sample. As various methodologies could produce inconsistent answers using the same data, confusion might happen. To get around the problem, a different method of determining normality that makes use of the 'skewness' and 'kurtosis' distribution may be used. It may be fairly accurate for both small and large samples (Kim, [2013](#page-16-21)). Skewness and Kurtosis are measurements of the asymmetry and peakedness of distribution, respectively. The majority of statistical software programmes include skewness and kurtosis values together with associated standard errors. To test for normality, 'skewness' and 'kurtosis' are used in conjunction with a 'z-test'. By dividing the 'skew values' or 'excess kurtosis' by their standard errors, a 'z-score' can be calculated. ## Conclusion

Traditionally, a single question or a simple 'yes' or 'no' response has been used to assess a student's overall satisfaction with their educational experience. However, based on a single question, students may not fully consider the other attributes of satisfaction. Accordingly, this quantitative study has focused on the role of schools' courtyard design and how it shapes student satisfaction using a survey composed of 32 questions/items. The measures of all variables and factors included in the study are tested for reliability using the inter-item consistency reliability test of Cronbach's alpha. The study illustrates an empirical investigation into how the design of schools' courtyards affects

students' satisfaction, and it has provided a better understanding of how the design of the school courtyard can affect student satisfaction.

Based on the statistical analysis, the study's statistical results indicate that Courtyard Design has a 37.2% chance of influencing Student Satisfaction, Courtyard Suitability has a 22.9% chance, and Courtyard Attractiveness has a 24.5% chance. In light of this, Courtyard Design should be regarded as a crucial structural component of the educational facility in addition to all other resources. In their study, (Uline & Tschannen-Moran, [2008)](#page-17-12) claimed that the physical environment of the school, including the characteristics of the buildings, facilities, and design, have an impact on the social atmosphere and, consequently, educational achievement and general satisfaction. Therefore, the educational administration should consider the school's courtyard as a physical component on an equal footing with other educational resources like the school curriculum and staff experience.

The suitability of the courtyard has also been statistically demonstrated to play a significant role in determining the satisfaction of the students. According to the questionnaire, 'courtyard suitability' refers to the perception of a roomy area ideal for playing and engaging in physical activity. As a conclusion, the courtyard area needs to be planned so that it can accommodate the number of students, allow for their freedom of movement, and minimize the stress that comes with crowded spaces. The courtyard needs to be large enough to serve as a recreation area, allowing the students to receive appropriate physical education that will ultimately result in physical fitness and mental well-being.

There is one more thing to note, the (Courtyard Attractiveness) which has also been perceived by the student as shaded areas for socializing and relaxation, thermal comfort, nice decoration and an area that gives a restful scene. This dimension has showed also a significant relationship with (Students Satisfaction). To improve air circulation within the courtyard, the courtyard should be designed from an architectural standpoint with the proper proportions in terms of length, width, and height in comparison to the adjacent masses.

The courtyard's proportions affect both its thermal conditions and the thermal conditions of the spaces around it. In addition to choosing the appropriate canopy material to enhance the shading spaces, the courtyard should be designed with the proper geometry to provide an adequate amount of shaded areas. Likewise, it is essential to stress the need for adequate cross ventilation when designing the courtyard to improve airflow and raise environmental comfort.

Courtyard Attractiveness incorporates also proper landscaping which significantly increases environmental comfort through evaporative cooling. Courtyard Attractiveness is related also to the improvement of the courtyard decoration.

The courtyard's integration of trees, flowers, and shrubs will make for pleasant scenes for the students, foster their creativity, enhance their imagination, and arouse their senses. The courtyard should be also decorated appropriately, with a nice seating area for relaxing, eating a break meal, and promoting social interaction among the students. The right colour choice for the courtyard walls and other areas should be given special consideration because it can greatly increase students' motivation to learn by inspiring them and encouraging them to think creatively.

Although the study's findings indicate a strong relationship between the courtyard's design and students' satisfaction, these findings should be viewed with some caution. The first limitation was that the sample of the study was only chosen from private schools and not from a mix of private and governmental schools because access to government schools required official special permission, and the procedure itself takes a lot of time. Another limitation was that only students in grades 9 through 12 were included in the sample; grades lower than 9 were excluded due to the possibility that they would not be able to understand the questionnaire. Future studies can therefore take these restrictions into account to target both the public and private sectors, as well as all academic levels. As for lower grades (less than grade 9), future studies may try to explore this link using a qualitative method, e.g. interviews or observation. Though the current study managed to establish a direct link between schools' courtyard design and student satisfaction, future studies may also try to study other variables/factors that may shape student satisfaction such as school climate, co-students and the psychological contract.
