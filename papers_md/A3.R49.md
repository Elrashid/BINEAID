## **The Infuence of Environmental, Social, and Governance ** **(ESG) Practices on US Firms' Performance: Evidence ** **from the Coronavirus Crisis**

**Ahmed Mohamed Habib****[1](http://orcid.org/0000-0003-1728-6028)** ** · Nahia Mourad2**

Received: 8 February 2022 / Accepted: 24 February 2023 / Published online: 18 March 2023 © The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2023

## **Abstract**

This study explores the infuence of total and individual ESG practices and the coronavirus crisis on US frm performance (FP). A large and recent sample of 406 US frms that adopted ESG issues during 2016–2020 was used. This study uses the generalized least-squares (GLS) regression estimator, the dynamic analysis technique, and robustness tests. The results indicate that frms with heightened ESG practices have better performance measures. In most cases, the results suggest that frms with heightened environmental, social, and governance performances have better performance measures. The results suggest that the coronavirus crisis negatively afected FP measures. In addition, the analyses of the diferences suggest signifcant distinctions in FP due to the coronavirus crisis. This study's fndings have important implications for stakeholders. Managers could beneft from the results of this examination by recognizing the status of ESG practices and FP before and during the coronavirus crisis and identifying the linkage between the fulfllment of ESG responsibilities and FP. This study provides noteworthy practical implications that could enable managers to develop strategies and policies for adopting and enhancing ESG practices to achieve the best performance. Furthermore, the results could infuence trading processes as investors and fnanciers pursue attractive fnancial returns from investments in businesses concerned with ESG issues.

## **Introduction**

The early 2000s fnancial crisis of the USA severely afected global markets, leading to economic obstacles that required signifcant levels of government intrusion and societal attention (Nicholson et al., [2011](#page-20-0)). Additionally, there have been many issues regarding ethical behavior, responsibility, supervision, and doubts regarding frm reporting as a reliable source of information for stakeholders (Alqallaf & Alareeni, [2018)](#page-18-0). Therefore, many frms seek to develop policies and strategies to provide information users with a transparent snapshot of their business responsibility procedures and initiatives by following environmental (ENV), social (SOC), and governance (GOV) criteria because of stress from ofcials, fnancial markets, and stakeholders (Habib, [2022](#page-19-0)). Enhanced transparency can help attract money and sustain investors' trust in the stock market. Simultaneously, a lack of disclosure may cause immoral behavior, manipulation, and doubt in exchange integrity, which costs frms, information users, and the economy (Demise, [2006](#page-19-1)). Consequently, frms attempt to disclose ESG issues to attract investments and enhance frm value (Habib, [2022](#page-19-0)).

ESG performance involves the integration of ENV, SOC, and GOV performance (Habib, [2022](#page-19-0)). Rezaee [(2016](#page-21-0)) confrms that including and employing ESG performance in the management strategy of frms can generate value. In recent years, ESG performance has attracted decision-makers as an essential method for enhancing frm value (Habib,[ 2022](#page-19-0); Malik, [2015)](#page-20-1). Therefore, some researchers believe that frms that desire to stand out in the business environment should prioritize enhancing their ESG performance (Habib,[ 2022](#page-19-0); Hockerts & Moir,[ 2004](#page-20-2); Vandekerckhove et al., [2008](#page-21-1)). Shiller ([2013)](#page-21-2) confrms that as fnancial markets play a crucial role in maintaining various SOC activities, ESG information also serves investors and society. Therefore, Eccles et al. ([2014)](#page-19-2) assert that disclosing ESG information to stakeholders is necessary for frms to attract long-term investors. In addition, ESG analysis provides a holistic view of the potential ENV and SOC risk areas and opportunities for frms in rapidly changing markets. Firms concentrating on ESG investments can reduce costs, enhance productivity, alleviate risk potential, deliver opportunities for revenue generation, and improve their earnings and long-term sustainability (Eccles et al., [2014;](#page-19-2) Malik,[ 2015](#page-20-1); Rezaee, [2016](#page-21-0)).

Several theories have proposed explanations for the ESG issues. For example, stakeholder theory posits that corporate SOC responsibility practices may help businesses enhance their relationships with stakeholders (Bitektine & Haack, [2015;](#page-19-3) Russo & Perrini, [2010;](#page-21-3) Tu & Huang, [2015](#page-21-4)). Institutional and legitimacy theories suggest that enhancing SOC practices improves FP (Beddewela & Fairbrass, [2016;](#page-19-4) Velte, [2017)](#page-21-5). In addition, resource allocation theory suggests that businesses should determine how to allocate resources to all productive activities in a cost-efective manner (Ferrier, [1994;](#page-19-5) Li & Cui, [2008)](#page-20-3). The resource-based theory implies that large frms perform better in ESG practices. From a resourcebased perspective, these resources enable frms to enhance their ESG practices (Ruf et al., [2001](#page-21-6)). Thus, it improves frm image and customer trust (Greening & Turban, [2000](#page-19-6); Orlitzky et al., [2003](#page-20-4)). Moreover, Godfrey et al. ([2009](#page-19-7)) suggest that

## **Data and Methodology**

The current study uses a large and recent sample of US frms. Table [1 ](#page-6-0)lists the sample size and characteristics, representing 403 frms with 2015 frm-year observations. These frms are listed on the NASDAQ Capital Market (NASDAQ-CM), New York Stock Exchange (NYSE), and NYSE American Exchange (NYSE-AM), whose annual data were obtained using Standard and Poor's DataStream from 2016 to 2020.

Many types of research are subjective regarding FP measurement, as each indicator concentrates on a unique component with inherent biases and limitations. Orlitzky et al. ([2003)](#page-20-4) argued that the indicators related to accounting measurements are essential metrics of frm efciency; nonetheless, investors care about past, current, and future market prices and frm market value. Their choices were based on the observations of these signs. Additionally, Ullmann ([1985)](#page-21-11) states that marketbased performance indicators are superior to indicators based on accounting because investors focus on a frm's capacity to generate future earnings instead of prior FP. Moreover, market-based performance indicators are unlikely to be infuenced by

accounting changes and management manipulation. These indicators are crucial to investors' decisions to buy stocks. Thus, this study uses market-based performance indicators as dependent variables, that is, market value (MV), total enterprise value (TEV), and Tobin's *Q* ratio, because accounting adjustments and management manipulations are unlikely to afect market-based performance metrics. Table [2 ](#page-7-0)presents the defnitions of the dependent, independent, and control variables.

To explore the infuence of total and individual ESG practices on market-based performance indicators, focusing on the coronavirus crisis's statistical infuence on frms' market value, we assign the study models as follows:

$$
DepVar_{i,t} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 ESG_{i,t} + \beta_2 COV_{i,t} + \beta_3 SIZE_{i,t} + \beta_4 AGE_{i,t} + \beta_5 LEV_{i,t} + \alpha (1)
$$

To further examine ESG performance, this study also examines the infuence of the sub-dimensions of ESG performance, that is, ENV, SOC, and GOV performance, as follows:

$$
DepVar_{i,t} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 ENV_{i,t} + \beta_2 SOC_{i,t} + \beta_3 GOV_{i,t} + \beta_4 COV_{i,t} + \beta_5 SIZE_{i,t} + \beta_6 AGE_{i,t} + \beta_7 LEV_{i,t} + \alpha (2)
$$

As mentioned in the above equations, the dependent variables are proxied by the MV, TEV, and Tobin's *Q* ratio. A natural log is considered for the variables to mitigate skewed data from being more normally distributed and to achieve a constant variance. Specifcally, MV is measured by multiplying a frm's outstanding ordinary shares by its current share price at the end of the period. TEV is measured as the sum of market capitalization, the market value of debt, and preferred stock after excluding cash and cash equivalents at the end of the period. Tobin's *Q* ratio is measured by dividing the market value of a frm by its total assets at the end of the period. ESG is the sum of the ENV, SOC, and GOV practice scores obtained from Standard and Poor's DataStream. The score is between 0 and 100, with the frst quartile (0–25) indicating poor relative ESG performance. The second quartile (> 25–50) indicates satisfactory relative ESG performance. The third quartile (> 50–75) indicates good relative ESG performance. The last quartile (> 75–100)

indicates excellent relative ESG performance. The sub-dimensions of ESG performance are interrelated and have the same importance because frms cannot concentrate on only one dimension to improve their overall ESG performance (Ahmad et al., [2021;](#page-18-3) Habib, [2022;](#page-19-0) Han et al.,[ 2016](#page-20-8); Sassen et al., [2016;](#page-21-9) Tarmuji et al., [2016;](#page-21-10) Velte, [2017)](#page-21-5). ENV, SOC, and GOV are the environmental, social, and governance performance scores, respectively. COV is measured by a dummy variable taking the value of one for the time of the coronavirus crisis and zero otherwise. The control variables considered are frm size (SIZE), age (AGE), and fnancial leverage (LEV). SIZE is measured as the natural log of a frm's total assets at the end of the period. AGE is measured as the natural log of a frm's age until the end of a period. LEV is measured by dividing a frm's debt by its assets at the end of the period.

This study used a generalized least-squares (GLS) regression estimator, which is considered a generalization of the ordinary least-squares (OLS) estimator and is used to obtain more accurate inferences for model parameters and overcome the issue of heteroscedasticity (González-Coya & Perron, [2022](#page-19-15); Hsiao, [2007](#page-20-13); Kaufman, [2013](#page-20-14)). Furthermore, this study adopted additional analyses using a dynamic analysis technique as a more general model. It is based on the Arellano-Bover/Blundell–Bond generalized method of moments (GMM) system estimator. This technique allows the dependent variable as a lagged variable included in the model to outline the dynamic efects in the panel data analysis, and it allows error terms to have autoregressive dependence over time (Ahmad et al., [2021;](#page-18-3) Elsayed & Paton, [2005](#page-19-9); Geroski et al., [1997)](#page-19-16). In addition, this study adopted robustness tests to verify the validity of the fndings.
